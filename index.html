Understanding Download vs. Upload Speed

Download speed refers to how fast your connection can pull data from the internet (watching Netflix, downloading files, browsing web pages).

Upload speed is the opposite: it's how fast you can send data out (posting to social media, sending emails, or video calling on Zoom).

Most home connections (DSL, Cable) are asymmetric, meaning download speeds are significantly higher than upload speeds. This is designed for typical consumers who consume more content than they upload.

How Our Upload Test Works

Our tool generates a small amount of browser-side data and measures the time required to send it to a remote endpoint. It is a best-effort estimate designed for quick checks rather than a precise ISP-grade throughput reading.

Technical Glossary

Latency (Ping) The time (in ms) for a data packet to travel to the server and back. Lower is better for gaming.
Jitter The variance in ping over time. High jitter causes buffering and lag spikes.
Upload Goal Min. 5-10 Mbps is recommended for stable HD video conferencing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about speed testing and network optimization.

Why is my internet slower than advertised?
Advertised speeds are usually "up to" maximums over a wired Ethernet connection. Wi-Fi interference, router distance, old hardware, and network congestion during peak hours can all reduce real-world results.
What is a good Ping (Latency) for gaming?
  • 0-20ms: Excellent. Pro-level gaming.
  • 20-50ms: Good. Standard for most gamers.
  • 50-100ms: Fair. Playable but may feel sluggish.
  • 100ms+: Poor. Noticeable lag will occur in fast-paced games.
Does changing DNS speed up internet?
Technically, no. Changing DNS does not increase your bandwidth. However, it can make browsing feel snappier by resolving website names faster, especially if your ISP DNS is slow or unreliable.
What is the difference between Mbps and MB/s?
ISPs sell speeds in Megabits (Mbps), but file downloads are shown in Megabytes (MB/s). There are 8 bits in 1 byte. So, if you have a 100 Mbps connection, your theoretical download speed is roughly 12.5 MB/s.
